# Recommendation offline evaluation

Generated by `dotnet NorthLife.Api.dll --evaluate-recommendations`.

Real traffic is too small to evaluate on, so the evaluation uses the persona simulator (see docs/features/recommendations.md). Simulated visitors with known tastes browse the catalogue for 30 days. The models are trained on the first 24 days and must predict which events each visitor opens in the last 6.

## Setup

- Catalogue: 192 events.
- Visitors: 1500 simulated visitors.
- Interactions: 59,713 for training, 22,364 for testing.
- Test users: 252 visitors who have training history and open at least one new event in the test period.
- Relevant events: those a visitor opened or navigated to in the test period and had not opened before.
- Averaging: every metric is averaged over visitors.
- Coverage: the share of the catalogue that appears in anyone's top 10.
- Diversity: the mean pairwise (1 − content cosine) inside a top-10 list.

## Tuning

The hyperparameters were tuned on a separate validation seed (7) and reported on the test seed (2026), so the numbers below were not tuned on the data they report.

The grid searched three settings:

- γ, the blend constant, with α = γ / (γ + shared visitors).
- μ, the weight of the popularity prior.
- The popularity percentile assumed for events nobody has seen yet.

Every event starts cold, so the objective values both cases equally:

J = NDCG@10 on known events ÷ the oracle's + NDCG@10 on new events ÷ the oracle's.

Chosen: **γ = 25, μ = 0.6, new-event prior = 0.9**. The best ten configurations:

## New events (item cold start)

A fifth of the catalogue is published only at the split: none of its interactions are in training, so no visitor has shared history with it. Relevant events here are only these new ones. The table covers 73 visitors who opened at least one.

Behaviour-based methods cannot see these events. The blend falls back to text, since with no shared visitors α = 1.
